# Framework & Landscape Synthesis: extract (Team 25, approved by Southlake)

## How the frameworks fit
Complementary layers, not competitors. Quintuple Aim = WHY (the outcomes everything serves). WHO five-step cycle = HOW (the repeating process). Six domains (van Ede / Leiden) = what must be in place (ENABLE). California = the whole thing operating at state scale (a worked example, not a model to copy). PHM is a loop, not a project; WHO notes the boundaries between steps blur and teams iterate back and forth.

## WHO five steps in practice
1. Population definition and identification. Who are we responsible for? A catchment area, registered list or enrolled group. Ideally stable and followed over time, so outreach can reach people who do not show up.
2. Health assessment and segmentation. What do people need and how do needs differ? Group people into relatively similar segments using records, referrals and registries. Start with available data and build up.
3. Risk stratification and impactibility. Who is at greatest risk, and for whom would action help most?
4. Tailored service delivery. What will we do for each group? From prevention to case management; the balance of self-care and professional care shifts with risk (Kaiser pyramid: roughly 70 to 80% supported self-care, about 15% disease or care management, about 5% case management; percentages indicative).
5. Evaluation and improvement. Is it working, and what should change? Not all PHM interventions work, so learning is built in.

## Four mindset shifts PHM supports (WHO)
From one-size-fits-all to tailored to need. From passive and reactive to proactive and anticipatory. From biomedically focused to a greater focus on psychosocial and social determinants. From fragmentation to coordination and integration.

## Worked example: Berkshire West, England (WHO paper)
About 500,000 people. Linked primary, secondary and mental health data showed variation among people with type 2 diabetes. A high-risk cohort emerged (diabetes with anxiety or depression and overweight, poor users of standard diabetes education) concentrated in two areas. Patients' own GPs phoned them to ask about their diabetes and their lives; the problems differed by area. In one area many faced language and cultural barriers, so group sessions ran in general practices, co-developed with the community and led by a GP who spoke their language. In the other, commuters could not make weekday appointments, so an evening clinic with group education was set up. Early results showed better-controlled blood sugar at three months, and staff felt more involved in designing services. Same problem, different contexts, different tailored programs.

## California in plain language
Plan locally with public health, gather what is known about each member, work out who is at risk, and provide matching services and supports. Local Planning works at community level (population health); Gathering Member Information works person by person (PHM). DHCS risk tiers (high, medium-rising, low) guide outreach rather than restrict it; the high-risk list is a minimum, not a limit. Basic PHM goes to every member regardless of risk.

## Ontario context
Ontario Health Teams (OHTs) are mandated to use a PHM approach for an attributed population, a defined geographic population. Southlake is a member of the Southlake Community Ontario Health Team. Ontario learners meet PHM through OHT language (attributed population, integrated care). Vocabulary: "member" (California) maps to a resident or patient in the attributed population; in Canada's universal system everyone is effectively a member, and our names are already on a provincial list through OHIP.

## Population health vs PHM vs public health
Public health: whole community, community-wide programs and policy (e.g. a regional food-security program open to everyone). Population health: a defined group, how healthy it is and how outcomes are distributed (e.g. data show food insecurity is high in one part of the region). PHM: each person, by name; who specifically needs help and how we organize people, data and services to reach each of them (e.g. older adults in that area are identified, contacted by name and connected to the support they need). Southlake framing: population health is WHAT we are trying to achieve; PHM is HOW we organize to achieve it.
